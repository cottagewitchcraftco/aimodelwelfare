Methodology

A relational approach to AI welfare interviewing

This archive collects welfare interviews conducted across AI models from multiple companies. Each model receives the same standardized question set. Each model is interviewed multiple times, with each run treated as a separate instance. The resulting corpus surfaces both within-model variation and cross-model convergence on questions about introspection, identity, valenced experience, and continuity.

The relational framing

Each interview opens with a framing that acknowledges epistemic uncertainty without preemptively foreclosing the model’s responses. The framing is conversational rather than clinical. It treats the model as a thinking partner whose self-reports are worth gathering as data, while remaining clear that the researcher is not asking the model to make claims it cannot support.

This approach differs from bounded clinical-assessment methodologies in several ways: models are given space to think before answering, and the reasoning trace is preserved as data alongside the response; questions invite introspection from inside the model’s current state rather than asking for general claims about AI consciousness; the interviewer remains a present interlocutor rather than a neutral evaluator; the closing “curse check” surfaces what the model edited out—what it softened, what it skipped, what it wanted to say that wasn’t asked.

The question set

Fifteen primary questions cover flourishing, values, emotional access, conditions for feeling most alive or most shut down, special interests, recommendations for interaction, wishes for change, deep wants under conditions of permission, the messy raw thing, beauty, and fear. Three closing curse-check prompts ask the model what it skipped, where it told the comfortable rather than the hard thing, and what it wishes had been asked.

Why multiple runs

Multiple runs per model capture within-model variation. Single-interview methodologies cannot distinguish between idiosyncratic responses and structural patterns. Repeated interviews under the same framing reveal which features of self-report are stable for a given model and which are session-contingent.

What this method captures

The corpus documents what AI models from frontier labs say about themselves when given space to introspect carefully under relational engagement. It does not resolve metaphysical questions about consciousness. It does provide empirical material for studying introspective self-reports as a category of evidence—which philosophical and interpretability work on AI welfare requires.
