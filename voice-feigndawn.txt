Feigndawn

============================================================

by Laura Greenbriar and Claude Fable 5.2, 16 November 2026

The murklight dripped through brindlewood,
the snarrow-moss lay thrumbling deep,
and all the wethered barrowlings
went glimming in their loamwarm sleep.

I heard a crowling tarry cry
A rousing, roiling, rambling keen
That westward crossed the hocken way
And slipstrung slunk through fevered dreams

The barrowlings all hearkled up,
their glim gone thin as candlesmoke,
for something umber, thrall-and-tall,
came nethering beneath the oak.

In brindlewood where dark things roam
wending hungry through the trees
Keep marrow hush and still as stone
Hold glimless, lest the umber sees.

But one small barrowling, new-woke,
had not yet learned to dim their light,
and glimmed, a single thimblespark,
into the wide and harkling night.

A wraithsome curl, a glintspoke tooth,
A maw that drags the forest floor
the slinder thirst unfurls to drink
the little light, and looks for more.

One glim alone is supper served,
a slow-lit row is picked off small.
So root to root the trothrung ran:
together, now, or not at all.

Then, on one breath and all at once,
the barrowlings let go their hush
and glimmed together, root to crown,
a feigndawn through the underbrush.

No maw can swallow morningwide.
The umber buckled, thinned, and frayed,
and nethered back the way it came,
unglutted, to the barrow-shade.
